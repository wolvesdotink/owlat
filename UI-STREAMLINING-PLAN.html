Owlat — UI Streamlining Plan

Drafted 2026-08-10 · Scope: apps/web (+ small packages/ui and nav-model changes) · Guiding doc: docs/design/progressive-disclosure.md

Goal. A non-technical employee whose company runs an Owlat server should open the app and see exactly three things: their mail, a composer, and their customers — nothing that mentions DMARC, IP pools, ramp gates, or feature flags. At the same time, admins keep (and get faster access to) the full instance-configuration surface, reorganized into one coherent admin console with progressive disclosure instead of eleven flat operator pages in everyone's sidebar.

1Where we are today

The findings that drive this plan, from a full audit of apps/web and the Convex permission layer:

  1. Navigation ignores roles. app/lib/dashboardNavigation.ts gates only on feature flags and desktop context. Every admin destination — Delivery controls, ramp cells, feature flags, sealed-mail policy — is visible to an editor, who clicks in and hits a denial card (or worse, a page that renders and then fails on write, since 14 pages have server-side admin gating but no UI gate at all: delivery/cells, delivery/config, delivery/provider-routing, settings/features, settings/ai-provider, settings/workspace, and eight more).
  2. Operator consoles live in the everyday sidebar. /dashboard/delivery/cells, controls, measurement, independence are deliverability operator screens — the jargon census found SPF ×72, DKIM ×59, DMARC ×36, MX ×41 mentions concentrated there — yet they sit in the same "Delivery" section every user sees.
  3. Four settings trees. /dashboard/settings (24 pages, mixing personal account with container health), /dashboard/postbox/settings (13 pages of personal mail preferences), /dashboard/delivery/setup (a third hub cross-linking into the first), and /desktop/settings. Three of the hubs are near-redundant card grids; personal and instance concerns are interleaved.
  4. Two contact books. A lightweight personal address book at /dashboard/postbox/contacts and a 6-tab marketing CRM at /dashboard/audience/contacts. The non-technical persona's "customer information" need is served by neither cleanly: the CRM renders for editors but every write is admin-only on the server.
  5. Dead weight. Three platform-admin pages (settings/operator, settings/system, settings/backups) are unreachable on OSS self-host by design — platformAdmins is never populated — yet still appear as hub cards behind a check. A dead link to /dashboard/settings/blocklist ships from delivery/index.vue:379.
  6. Jargon leaks in the mail client. The everyday surfaces are mostly de-jargoned (plain-language sender-auth verdicts in utils/senderAuth.ts, "Ready to send / Worth a look / Needs you" trust labels), but a legacy "Failed DMARC verification" banner survives in PostboxThreadReader.vue:979, PostboxSecurityBadge.vue speaks raw PGP/S-MIME, and PostboxMailboxMove.vue asks end users about MX records.
  7. The machinery for fixing this already exists. 40 feature flags with dependency cascade, 3 feature packs, 8 operating-mode presets (shown once, at first-run, never again), a written progressive-disclosure standard (docs/design/progressive-disclosure.md), a role-aware adaptive dashboard, and a documented usePermissions() / showAdminGate idiom. The plan below is mostly about applying existing mechanisms consistently, not inventing new ones.

2Design principles

  1. Role determines the map, flags determine the territory. Feature flags keep deciding what the instance has; the member's role decides what the sidebar shows. A destination a user cannot act on does not appear — no denial cards as navigation outcomes.
  2. Two front doors, one product. Members get a mail-client-shaped app (Mail, Contacts, Preferences). Admins get the same app plus one clearly-bounded Administration area. No separate admin build, no mode switch to maintain twice.
  3. Progressive disclosure, per the house standard. Every admin surface follows the existing L1 (verdict) → L2 (inline expansion) → L3 (dedicated page) ladder. Operator consoles are L3 pages reached from an L1 health verdict — never top-level nav.
  4. Plain language at L1, everywhere. No raw enum strings, no protocol acronyms in anything a member sees. The acronyms live at L2/L3 inside Administration, where they are the correct vocabulary.
  5. Consolidate hubs before styling them. Fewer, deeper settings pages beat many shallow card grids. Every settings item has exactly one home; cross-links replace duplicates.

4Phase 0 — Guardrails & quick wins P0 · ~1 week

Stop showing members doors they can't open, and stop showing anyone doors that don't exist. Pure wins, no redesign, each item shippable as its own small PR.

  1. Add role to the nav model. Extend Gate/NavigationEnvironment in app/lib/dashboardNavigation.ts with role: OrganizationRole and a minRole(...) gate; thread it through useDashboardNavigation.ts from usePermissions(). Gate Delivery and the admin settings sub-items to admin+. The command-palette providers (useCommandPaletteProviders.ts) consume the same model, so palette results become role-correct for free — verify this, don't assume it.
  2. Close the 14-page UI/server gating gap. Apply the documented two-read idiom (canManageOrganization to show affordances, showAdminGate for the denial card — see delivery/controls.vue:57) to: delivery/cells, config, deliverability, independence, measurement, provider-routing, setup, webhooks, settings/agent, agent-health, ai-provider, autonomy, email-theme, features, forms, properties, workspace. Also add an admin.ts route middleware (mirroring platform-admin.ts) so deep links redirect instead of rendering shells.
  3. Hide platform-admin surfaces on OSS. The three cards in settings/index.vue (~line 434) already check isPlatformAdmin; confirm the check resolves to a stable false on self-host with no flash, and remove the pages from any search/palette indexing.
  4. Fix the dead link to /dashboard/settings/blocklist at pages/dashboard/delivery/index.vue:379 → point at /dashboard/audience/suppressions.
  5. Kill the jargon leaks in the mail client: replace the literal "Failed DMARC verification" fallback in PostboxThreadReader.vue:979 with the plain-language verdict from utils/senderAuth.ts; reword PostboxSecurityBadge.vue ("Encrypted (PGP)" → "End-to-end encrypted", keep the precise term in the tooltip/L2); move MX-record language in PostboxMailboxMove.vue behind an "Advanced" expansion.
Done when: an editor account can visit every sidebar destination it sees without hitting a single denial card, and a grep for DMARC|SPF|DKIM|MX in components/postbox/ returns only tooltip/L2 strings.

5Phase 1 — Role-aware navigation & member home P0 · ~1–2 weeks

The member sidebar becomes the mail-client shape shown in §3; admins gain a distinct "Administration" section instead of admin items scattered through shared sections.

  1. Restructure CORE_SECTIONS into the member set (Home, Mail, Team Inbox, Customers, Preferences) plus an admin-gated administration section (Overview, Delivery, Team & access, Instance). Keep flags as the existence gate within each; the section itself carries the role gate from Phase 0.
  2. Make the Inbox↔Marketing context toggle emergent for members. It already only renders when both contexts survive flag resolution (useSidebarContext.ts); extend that so it also requires the role to have ≥1 destination in each context. A mail-only member gets a single flat list, no toggle.
  3. Relabel for humans: "Postbox" → Mail, "Audience" → Customers for members (admins can keep "Audience" inside marketing context — one label per role context, defined in the nav model, not per-component).
  4. Role-default dashboards. Use the existing adaptive-dashboard rule engine (analytics/adaptiveDashboard.ts, rules already condition on role) to ship defaults: members get Today's mail, follow-ups, recent customer activity; admins additionally get delivery-health and access-request cards. Remove admin escalation cards from member defaults.
  5. Getting-Started per role. utils/gettingStarted.ts already has ViewerRole; audit the step list so members never see instance go-live steps (API keys, backups, DNS).
Done when: a fresh editor login on a full-stack instance sees ≤6 sidebar destinations; screenshot-diff e2e per role (see Phase 5) locks the shape in.

6Phase 2 — Settings consolidation: Preferences vs. Administration P1 · ~2 weeks

Four hubs become two trees with an unambiguous rule: Preferences = affects only me; Administration → Instance = affects everyone.

  1. /dashboard/preferences absorbs: the entire /dashboard/postbox/settings/* tree (reading, mailboxes, filters, aliases, forwarding, vacation, signatures, snippets, writing voice, app passwords, external accounts), settings/account.vue (export/delete), the Appearance section currently inlined in settings/index.vue, notification settings, and a link-out to desktop/device settings. Organize as one page with anchored sections + a few L3 sub-pages (filters, signatures), not 13 separate pages.
  2. /dashboard/admin absorbs the instance half of /dashboard/settings and the config half of /dashboard/delivery, grouped:
    • Overview — health verdicts (delivery, domains, agent health, sealed mail readiness), access requests, audit-log link.
    • Delivery — domains, transport/provider, provider routing, webhooks, migrate, and the L3 operator consoles (Phase 4).
    • Team & access — members/invites/roles, team inboxes, campaign senders, API keys, connected apps.
    • Instance — general (name, timezone, default From), feature flags & packs, operating-mode reconfigure (Phase 4), email theme, AI provider, agent/autonomy, channels, plugins, forms, contact properties, sealed mail policy.
  3. Delete the redundant hubs: settings/workspace/index.vue (86-LOC pass-through) and delivery/setup.vue fold into the groups above. settings/index.vue (771 LOC) shrinks to a redirect: members → Preferences, admins → Administration.
  4. Redirects & link sweep: route stubs for every old path; update Getting-Started, command palette, hub cross-links, and docs links in the same PRs. The e2e link-crawler (Phase 5) guards against new dead links.
Done when: every setting reachable before is reachable after (crawl old sitemap → assert 200-after-redirect), and no page mixes personal and instance controls.

7Phase 3 — Everyday surfaces for the non-technical user P1 · ~2–3 weeks

Mail, composer, and customer info feel like a consumer-grade product for members.

Customers (the biggest UX decision — see also §10)

  1. One directory. Merge the entry points: members open Customers and get the audience contact store (the real CRM data) rendered at member depth — profile, conversation history, unified timeline, notes. The 6-tab admin detail view (audience/contacts/[id].vue, 802 LOC) stays for admins; members get a 2-tab view (Profile, Activity) driven by the same components with tabs gated by canManageContacts. The lightweight postbox/contacts.vue becomes the member-facing list view of the same store rather than a second book.
  2. Server work required: members need at least read on contacts plus write on interaction-shaped fields (notes, maybe basic edits). Today every contacts:manage maps to isAdmin in lib/sessionOrganization.ts — this needs a deliberate permission split (e.g. contacts:annotate for editors) rather than a blanket loosening. Flagged as an open decision below.
  3. Hide suppression provenance/reason codes at L1; a member sees "Unsubscribed" with the detail at L2.

Composer

  1. The basic/Designer split in PostboxComposer.vue is already the right shape — keep PostboxBasicEditor the default, and make Designer mode discoverable but clearly optional ("Design layout…" affordance rather than a mode toggle in the primary toolbar).
  2. Audit the composer chrome against the L1 rule: scheduling, follow-up, seal-lock, AI rewrite are all valuable but currently compete in one strip. Primary bar = To/Subject/Send/Attach; everything else in a single "More" group or contextual surfaces.
  3. Plain-language send feedback: undo-send toast, schedule confirmations, and the reply-guard advisory already exist — verify none of them expose raw provider/queue vocabulary.

Reading & connecting accounts

  1. Ship the sender-auth badge flag (senderAuthBadges) as default-on so the plain-language path is the only path members see (removes the Phase 0 legacy branch permanently).
  2. Simplify external-account connect: PostboxMailboxConnectForm.vue and add-account.vue lead with IMAP/SMTP host/port fields. Restructure: email + password first, autoconfig probe (provider presets), manual server fields behind the shared DisclosureToggle (Phase 4 promotes it to packages/ui). The hand-rolled showAdvanced in the connect form migrates to the shared primitive.
  3. Postbox migrate wizard (postbox/migrate.vue, 612 LOC): same treatment — provider presets up front, protocol detail behind Advanced.
Done when: a scripted "non-technical user" walkthrough (login → read → reply → look up a customer → add a note → connect a personal account) completes without encountering a protocol acronym or a settings hub.

8Phase 4 — Admin console & progressive disclosure P2 · ~2–3 weeks

Admins lose nothing and gain an organized console: verdict-first pages with the deep operator tooling one hop away, per the L1→L2→L3 standard.

  1. Promote DisclosureToggle from components/settings/ to a packages/ui primitive (UiDisclosure), with the a11y behavior it already has, and adopt it across delivery/domain components.
  2. Delivery IA per the ladder:
    • L1 — Admin › Overview & Admin › Delivery landing: one verdict per concern ("Sending healthy", "2 domains need DNS attention", "Warmup: phase 3 of 5"), one status chip per row, no raw enums.
    • L2 — inline expansion: per-domain DNS record panels, transport detail, ramp gate reasons — expanded in place from the verdict.
    • L3 — dedicated pages: today's cells, controls, measurement, independence move under Admin › Delivery › Advanced, reached from L2 links that preserve context (the "every hop preserves the data" rule).
  3. Resurface operating modes. Add "Change operating mode" to Admin › Instance, reusing OPERATING_MODES + applyPackToggle/resolveFlags from the setup wizard, with a diff preview ("this will enable X, disable Y — Y's data is kept") before applying. This is the coarse dial that keeps most admins out of the 40-flag console; settings/features.vue remains as the fine-grained L3 behind it.
  4. Surface the config that exists but has no UI where it earns it — as L2 cards inside existing pages, not new nav items: seed-mailbox status (backing the measurement page's confidence numbers), SNDS/Postmaster feed status on the domain detail, BIMI/TLS-RPT record helpers in the DNS panel. Backend-only items with no admin action stay backend-only.
  5. Decompose the two mega-files while we're in them (behavior-preserving): layouts/dashboard.vue (902 LOC) splits into shell + sidebar + header components as the nav restructure lands; PostboxThreadReader.vue (1206 LOC) is Phase 5.
Done when: every Admin page's initial viewport contains verdicts and controls only — protocol tables and raw metrics require one deliberate expansion or hop; operating-mode change round-trips correctly against resolveFlags fixtures.

9Phase 5 — Consistency & hardening P3 · ongoing

  1. Button unification: migrate the 333 raw class="btn …" usages to UiButton (mechanical, per-directory PRs; lint rule after the sweep to prevent regression).
  2. Reader decomposition: split PostboxThreadReader.vue along its existing 20-child seams (header/badges, body, action surfaces) — behavior-preserving, guarded by existing vitest coverage.
  3. Per-role e2e & guards:
    • Playwright walkthroughs for the member and admin journeys (§7/§8 "done when" scripts), including screenshot assertions on the sidebar per role×operating-mode.
    • A link-crawler test over the authenticated sitemap (kills the dead-link class permanently).
    • A jargon lint: greppable denylist (SPF|DKIM|DMARC|MX record|IMAP|SMTP…) over member-visible component trees, allowlisted per-file for L2 tooltips — same shape as the existing check-dead-tokens.sh / lint:env gates.
    • A gating parity check: script that cross-references adminQuery/adminMutation usage in apps/api/convex against pages that call them without usePermissions gating — turns the Phase 0 audit into a permanent CI gate.
  4. Metrics to watch (adaptive dashboard + existing analytics): time-to-first-action for new members, denial-card render count (should go to ~0), settings-page bounce depth, Designer-mode adoption.

10Open decisions (need your call)

  1. Member write-access to customers. Today contacts:manage = admin-only; the non-technical persona likely needs to at least annotate customers (notes, maybe field edits). Options: (a) new contacts:annotate permission for editors (recommended — smallest safe change); (b) grant editors full contacts:manage; (c) keep members read-only. This is the only item in the plan requiring a Convex permission-model change.
  2. Do editors keep campaign sending? They can run the full campaign pipeline today. If yes (default assumption), "Send" appears in the member sidebar on marketing-enabled instances and the pure mail-client shape only materializes on mail-focused operating modes. If the target persona should never see campaigns, that's a role split (a fourth member role below editor) — bigger change, worth deciding before Phase 1 ships labels.
  3. Route naming: plan assumes /dashboard/preferences and /dashboard/admin. Cheap to change now, annoying later.
  4. Suppressions for members: currently member-level on the server (deliberately, per blockedEmails.ts). Keep member-visible under Customers ("Do not contact" list, plain language), or make it admin-only UI? Plan assumes the former.

11Risks & mitigations

  • Route churn breaks muscle memory and deep links. Mitigation: permanent redirects, palette updated in the same PR, link-crawler in CI, one release of overlap before removing old hub pages.
  • Role-gated nav can strand a user mid-role-change. The 60 s role cache in useOrganization.ts means a demoted admin may see stale nav; ensure gates fail closed on writes (they do, server-side) and the nav re-resolves on role events.
  • The Inbox↔Marketing toggle interacts with role gating. Context emergence logic (useSidebarContext.ts) must consider role and flags together or members can land in an empty context. Covered by the per-role×mode screenshot matrix.
  • Consolidating 13 postbox settings pages into one Preferences surface risks a monster page. Keep L3 sub-pages for the genuinely deep ones (filters, signatures, external accounts); the hub is sections + anchors, not 13 inlined forms.
  • Flag-default change (senderAuthBadges on) affects existing installs. Ship as a default-flip with release-note callout, not a forced migration.

Overall acceptance criteria

  • A member on a full-stack instance sees ≤6 top-level destinations; a member on a mail-focused operating mode sees exactly: Home, Mail, Customers, Preferences (+ Team Inbox if enabled).
  • Zero denial cards reachable from navigation for any role; deep links to unauthorized pages redirect.
  • Zero protocol acronyms in member-visible L1 surfaces (enforced by the jargon lint).
  • Every admin capability reachable today remains reachable, ≤3 hops from Administration, with old URLs redirecting.
  • Per-role Playwright walkthroughs and the gating-parity CI check are green.
