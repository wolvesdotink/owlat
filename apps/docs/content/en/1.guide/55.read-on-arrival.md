With the **`ai`** feature flag on (it needs an LLM provider configured, see [Feature flags](/guide/feature-flags)), Postbox reads new mail in your personal mailboxes when it arrives, not when you open it. By the time you look, each conversation already has its brief, and the inbox is sorted into what needs you, what is just an update, and what can wait for your digest.

Team inboxes are not affected. They keep their own [Team Inbox](/guide/team-inbox) pipeline.

::callout{type="info" title="Your AI provider reads your mail"}
Reading on arrival means your AI provider sees personal mail as soon as it is delivered, including mail you never open. You can turn it off; see [Turning it off](#turning-it-off).
::

## How much of a mail is read

Not every mail gets the same read. Mail that may need something from you is read in full, as before. Mail that only informs you is read as little as needed:

| Kind of mail                                                                                       | How it is read                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mail from a person, and anything that may need a reply                                             | In full: the brief, open items, what you are waiting for                                             |
| Notifications and receipts                                                                         | A short quote (the gist): one or two sentences copied word for word from the mail, never paraphrased |
| Newsletters and promotions                                                                         | Counted only, no model call. Their lines come from one call when your digest is put together         |
| Security mail (sign-in codes, password resets), and senders you auto-archived or unsubscribed from | Not read                                                                                             |

Mail that carries payment details, changed bank details, an invoice with payment details or a legal notice is read in full instead of as a gist or a count, so **Read the exact wording** works for it as it does for any other mail. This holds even when the sender is in your digest, a rule sends it there, or it looks like a newsletter or promotion. It is still only counted when the AI budget is used up or a lot of mail arrives at once (see below), or when you sent this conversation to the digest yourself with **Fix** after this mail arrived. An older **Fix** of the conversation does not count it.

When the AI budget is used up, or a lot of mail arrives at once, informational mail is counted instead of read. Mail that may need you is never lowered that way; it waits and is read in full.

## Where mail lands

The inbox's Priority view groups conversations by where they landed:

| Group                         | What lands here                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Needs you**                 | Conversations that ask you something and wait for your reply, together with the [Reply Queue](/guide/reply-queue) |
| **Everything else**           | Mail from people, and anything not sorted into the groups below                                                   |
| **Updates · no reply needed** | Notifications and receipts. The first three show; the rest fold into one line                                     |
| **In your digest**            | Newsletters and promotions, as one line that opens them in All mail                                               |

Mail that carries payment details, changed bank details, an invoice with payment details or a legal notice never lands in **Updates** or **In your digest**, whatever the sender, a rule or the kind of mail says: it shows under **Everything else** at least, so it cannot be overlooked. Your own choices still win: a place you picked for the conversation with **Fix** after this mail arrived, and senders you auto-archive or unsubscribed from. A **Fix** you made before it arrived was about the earlier mail of the conversation, so it does not hide this one.

Nothing is moved or deleted to build these groups. Only mail from senders you chose to auto-archive or unsubscribe from is filed to Archive. A conversation that has not been sorted yet shows where it always did.

## Why here? and Fix

Every sorted conversation has a **Why here?** link: in the strip at the top of the thread, in the header's **⋯** menu, and on a row of the Priority view when you hover it. It shows what decided the place (your choice for this conversation or sender, how you handle the sender, one of your rules, the kind of mail, or payment or legal wording that keeps it out of the digest), how much of the mail was read, how many model calls it took, and how many mails from the sender arrived in about 90 days and how many of them you opened.

- **That's wrong** opens **Fix**. Pick where mail like this should go (Needs you, Everything else, Updates, Digest, or Archive automatically) and whether that applies to **This conversation** or to **Everything from** the sender. Fix never changes the sender's category for other features.
- **Always here** keeps all mail from the sender where this one is.
- **Senders page** opens the [Senders](#senders) page.

Fix has an optional **Why?** box. If you write a reason, Owlat turns it into a draft rule that waits in [Rules in your words](#rules-in-your-words). It is never switched on by itself.

A sender-wide Fix re-sorts the sender's newest 200 conversations in the inbox. **Archive automatically** for a sender also archives their recent mail in the inbox and says how many were moved.

## Learning from what you archive

If you keep archiving a sender's mail without opening it (five mails, on at least three separate occasions), Postbox sends that sender's mail to your digest from then on and tells you: "Mail from X now goes to your digest", with an **Undo**. It only learns from mail a person archived unread. Rules, Fix and automatic archiving never count.

Learning never overrides a choice you made yourself. If you undo a learned change, it is not learned again until you change that sender by hand. Replying to a sender that was learned into the digest puts it back where it was.

## Undo

Every change Postbox makes on its own or on your behalf (a Fix, a sender status, a learned digest, a rule that filed or labelled a mail, a delayed archive, applied suggestions) is recorded, and offers **Undo** in its notice. Undo puts back only what is still the way the change left it: if you have since moved a mail yourself, that mail stays where you put it. Reply reminders an archive cleared are not restored, and the notice says so. Changes can be undone for 30 days.

## Senders

**Senders** in the folder rail lists who writes to you and what Postbox does with their mail. It replaces the old Subscriptions page; old links lead here.

- **Tabs:** To review, All, Digest, Auto-archived, Unsubscribed.
- **Columns:** the sender, how many mails arrived in about 90 days, how many of them you opened, the kind of mail, and a control for what happens to new mail.

| Choice           | What happens to new mail from the sender          |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Keep**         | It lands as usual                                 |
| **Digest**       | It stays in the inbox and goes to your digest     |
| **Auto-archive** | It is archived and marked read when it arrives    |
| **Unsubscribe**  | It is archived, and Owlat asks the sender to stop |

Your own filters run first. A mail a filter already moved is left alone.

**Unsubscribe** archives the sender's mail at once, whatever happens next. For a sender with one-click unsubscribe, Owlat sends the request from the server and shows whether it worked. For other senders it opens their unsubscribe page or a prepared mail, and you finish there.

**To review** lists senders you rarely read. **Apply N suggestions** offers: Digest for a sender with five or more mails in about 90 days that you read less than 10% of, and Unsubscribe for promotions you never opened (Auto-archive for those without one-click unsubscribe). You confirm the list before anything changes, and it is undone as one change.

When Postbox has paused AI drafts for a sender because you kept discarding them, the row says **Drafts paused** with a **Resume** button.

In an open conversation, **Shift+E** auto-archives the sender's mail and **Shift+O** unsubscribes (with the same confirmation as the Unsubscribe chip).

## Rules in your words

At the top of **Settings → Filters**, **Rules in your words** lets you describe a rule in plain language, for example "Invoices from suppliers go to Finance". Owlat turns the text into a rule and shows it to you as conditions and actions. Nothing runs until you save it.

These rules only file, label, star, mark read, pin to a section, place a mail (Digest, Updates or Needs you), archive it later, or notify you. They never forward, delete or reply. Moves to Trash or Spam are refused.

**Test on recent mail** runs a rule against your last 50 inbox mails and shows which ones it would match, and why. Saved rules apply to new mail only.

### AI questions

A rule can ask a question only AI can answer, such as "is this about a trip I booked?". The question is asked once, when a mail arrives, together with the check of what kind of mail it is. A mail waits for at most five such questions; rules further down are skipped for it. An unclear answer counts as no. Rules with an AI question never run on older mail.

### Archive later

A rule can archive a mail after a number of hours (1 to 720). It is not archived if you reply in the conversation, star the mail, or move it in the meantime.

**Archive codes and sign-in links after a day** is a separate switch on the same page, off by default. One-time codes and sign-in links then leave the inbox 24 hours after they arrive, unless you star them or reply.

## The digest

Turn on **Daily brief by email** in Postbox settings to get the digest. Your daily brief then gets a digest section with Newsletters, Updates and Promotions: up to five lines per group, each quoting the mail and linking to it, and "and N more" for the rest. A mail is included in one digest only; new mail in the same conversation goes into the next one. If nothing else is new, the brief is still sent when the digest has something in it.

## Turning it off

**Read new mail when it arrives** in [Postbox settings](/guide/postbox-settings) turns reading on arrival off for you. New mail is then still checked for replies and categories, and the brief of other mail is built when you open it, as before. Each person decides this for themselves.

After you connect a mailbox and its history import finishes, Postbox also prepares your active conversations from the last 30 days, once, while this setting is on. It uses the AI budget, pauses when that is spent, and you can stop it in Postbox settings. Mailboxes connected earlier are not prepared automatically.
