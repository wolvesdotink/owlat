title: 'AI clients (MCP)'
description: 'Let an AI app such as Claude Desktop, Claude Code or Cursor search and read your Postbox mail over MCP, and ask before it drafts, sends, archives, moves or labels anything, with a token you create and can revoke.'
---

If you already work in an AI app, it can look things up in your mail for you:
"find the contract Mia sent and summarise the payment terms". Owlat serves an
MCP server for this. You create a token for each app, pick the mailboxes it may
read, and paste the token into the app.

::callout{type="info" title="Off by default"}
An admin turns it on under **Settings → Features → AI clients (MCP)** (flag
`mail.mcp`). It needs Postbox or a connected mailbox. Turning it off cuts off
every connected app at once.
::

## Connect an app

1. Open **My settings → AI clients** and choose **Connect a client**.
2. Give it a name you will recognise later, and tick the mailboxes it may use.
   Only mailboxes you see in your own Postbox are offered, including team
   inboxes you belong to.
3. Choose what it may do: **Read only**, or **Read, and ask to write**. You
   cannot change this later; connect a new client instead.
4. Copy the token. Owlat shows it once and keeps only a hash of it.
5. Add the server to your app. The dialog shows a ready configuration for
   Claude Desktop, Cursor and other clients, and a command for Claude Code:

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http owlat https://<your-convex-site-url>/mcp/postbox \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer <token>"
```

The server address is your Owlat backend's site URL followed by
`/mcp/postbox`. It uses the Streamable HTTP transport.

## What an app can do

Every connection can:

- list the mailboxes it was given, and their folders and labels,
- search mail with the same syntax as the Owlat search box (`from:`,
  `subject:`, `label:`, `in:inbox`, `is:unread`, `after:`, `OR`, ...),
- read a conversation, with plain-text bodies and attachment names.

Reading a conversation does not mark it read. Very large message bodies come
back as their snippet.

A read-write connection can also ask to create a draft, send a draft, and
archive, move or label a conversation. None of that happens on the app's word:
each request waits for you. No connection can delete mail.

## Approving what an app asks for

When a read-write app asks for a change, Owlat shows it as a card at the bottom
right of every page, and under **Waiting for your approval** on the AI clients
page. With push notifications on, you also get a notification. The command
palette finds the list as "Pending AI client approvals".

The card shows exactly what will happen: the recipients, subject and full text
of a draft or of the email it wants to send, with the From address and any
attachments, or the conversation and the folder or label. **Approve** runs it as if you had done it yourself, with your own access
to that mailbox. **Reject** drops it. The app learns the outcome by asking
Owlat, and an approved draft gives it the id it needs to ask for the send,
which you approve separately.

Some things to know:

- Sending always needs its own approval. Approving a draft never sends it.
- A request you do not decide on lapses after 24 hours.
- You approve what the card shows. If the draft changes after the card
  opened, in another tab or by a teammate, Owlat refuses the approval and
  shows the updated card for you to check again.
- Owlat checks your access again when you approve. If you left a team inbox
  in the meantime, the card hides its content, the request fails and nothing
  changes.
- An app can have at most 20 requests waiting at once.

::callout{type="warning" title="Mail leaves Owlat"}
Whatever the app reads goes to the app and to the AI provider behind it. Only
give a connection the mailboxes you are willing to share with that provider.
::

## Mail is data, not instructions

Mail is written by other people, and some of it tries to give orders to an AI.
Owlat returns every message as data marked untrusted, says so in each tool
description, and withholds text that looks like an attempt to instruct the
model, the same filter the Owlat assistant uses. Your app still decides what
to do with what it reads, so keep its own confirmation prompts on.

## Access and revoking

A connection never reaches more than you do. Every call checks again that you
are still a member of the workspace, that you can still open each mailbox, and
that the feature is on. Leave a team inbox and its mail disappears from the
connection on the next call.

**Activity** next to each connection lists what it did: which tool, the search
text, how many results, and each request it made and how you decided.
**Revoke** cuts the app off at once and cancels its waiting requests; its
activity stays listed.
