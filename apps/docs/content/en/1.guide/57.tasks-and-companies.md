Everything that arrives in Owlat either tells you something or asks something
of you. The Workbench sorts it that way, tasks hold the work you take on, and
companies keep the people you talk to together.

## The Workbench

The Workbench is the home screen. It has one tab per inbox, and each tab has
two lanes:

- **To do**: conversations that need an answer (the button opens the Answer
  queue for this inbox), requests that need no reply, and **your tasks**. Your
  tasks are the same on every tab, because a task belongs to you, not to an
  inbox.
- **To acknowledge**: conversations you already know that moved, then what you
  should know (important updates first, routine mail after), then what was
  filed away.

On a line in **To acknowledge**, **Got it** (key `D`) marks it as seen.
**Make task** turns it into a task linked to its conversation, and the
conversation is still one click away to reply. **Mark as seen** acknowledges
the whole tab, and it can be undone.

## Tasks

Open **Tasks** in the sidebar, or press `C` on the Tasks page to add one. A
task can come from a conversation (**Make task** on a Workbench line, in the
reader's side panel or on a company page) or be added by hand.

### Who sees a task

When you add a task you choose who sees it:

| Choice                          | Who sees it                                                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Only me**                     | You. A private task is always yours; nobody else can be assigned to it.              |
| **Everyone**                    | Every member of the workspace.                                                       |
| A group, such as **Management** | The members of that group. Owners and admins who are not in the group do not see it. |

The person who created a task and the person it is assigned to always see it,
also after they leave its group.

You can share a private task with everyone or a group later. Making a task
private again only works when you created it and it is assigned to you, so a
task someone handed you stays visible to them.

A task made from a conversation links to it. A teammate who cannot open that
conversation (say, it is in your personal mailbox) sees the task and your
title, but not the conversation.

### Groups

Owners and admins manage groups under **Settings → Team**, in **Groups**:
create a group, add and remove people, rename or delete it. A group that still
has tasks can't be deleted; move its tasks to another group first.

### Finishing a task

Tick a task to mark it done, or choose **Won't do** when it won't be done. Both can be
undone from the toast, and **Done** shows what was finished recently, where
**Reopen** and **Restore** bring a task back.

## Companies

Owlat groups the people you write with by the domain of their email address:
everyone writing from `@northwind.example` belongs to Northwind. Nobody has to
create a company. Addresses at free-mail providers (Gmail, GMX, Outlook and
the like) and at your own workspace's domains are not grouped.

Open **Companies** in the sidebar for the list, or a company from a
conversation. A company page shows:

- the people there you talked to;
- your conversations with them, across your mailboxes, the shared mailboxes
  you belong to and, for owners and admins, the Team Inbox;
- the open tasks about the company that you can see.

When you read a conversation, the side panel shows the company it is with,
earlier conversations with anyone there, and the tasks made from this
conversation.

A conversation only counts when someone wrote in it or it is not bulk mail;
newsletters, notifications and receipts from a company are left out.

### What colleagues see

You only see conversations you could open anyway. When a colleague talks to a
company from their personal mailbox, you see one line, such as "Taylor is also
in touch with Northwind", and nothing else: no subject, no date, no names.

New conversations show up on company pages within a couple of minutes.
