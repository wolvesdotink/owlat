---
title: "Reply Queue"
description: "A task list of emails waiting on your reply — Postbox detects unanswered asks, ranks them by urgency and age, and clears them the moment you respond."
---

The Reply Queue turns "emails I still owe an answer" into a task list. Postbox watches your personal mail for messages that are waiting on **you** — addressed to you directly, from a real person (not bulk/no-reply mail), with no later reply from you in the thread — and lists them in one place, ranked by urgency and then by how long they've been waiting.

The Reply Queue is part of the **Answer queue**, which also holds the team inbox's agent drafts and chat mentions. Open it from **Answer queue** in the sidebar (it carries a count badge), from **Answer queue** in a Postbox folder rail (filtered to that inbox), or from the "waiting on your reply" strip at the top of the inbox while there is mail in the queue. The strip can be dismissed for the session. Chips at the top of the queue narrow it to one inbox.

## What the queue shows

The queue takes its items one at a time.

- **Mail waiting on your reply** opens straight in Answer mode: the conversation on one side, your reply on the other. The top bar shows the subject, the inbox the reply goes out from, and your place in the queue ("1 of 5", with arrows to the previous and next item).
- **A follow-up reminder** (see Reply reminders below) stays on the queue page as a card, because here you're the one waiting. The card reads "You're waiting on &lt;name&gt;" under a **Follow-up** chip, with the latest snippet from the thread.

Above a card, the queue page shows a "1 of 5" counter and a time estimate for what's left, and a muted line underneath names the next item. With AI enabled, that name is a one-line summary of what they're asking. Without AI, or if the AI call fails, it's the message subject.

## Actions

In Answer mode:

- **Send** the reply. That finishes the item, and the next one opens.
- **Draft with AI** writes a starter reply into the editor. Type what the reply should say into the "What should the reply say?" field if you like (it is optional), then press `Enter` or click **Draft with AI**. `Cmd+J` or `Ctrl+J` jumps to that field. You always review and send yourself.
- The **in Answer queue** menu in the top bar finishes an item without a reply: **Archive** (also `e`), **Snooze** (also `h`) and **Mark done**.
- `[` and `]` move to the previous and next item without finishing anything.

On a follow-up card:

- **Done** (also `Enter`) dismisses the reminder.
- **Reply** opens Answer mode to write a nudge. The item stays in the queue until you send it.
- **Open** shows the thread in Postbox.
- `←` and `→` (or `k` and `j`) browse.

The queue keeps itself honest. Replying to a thread from anywhere (webmail or a connected IMAP client), archiving it or trashing it all clear the item live, with no manual refresh. **Undo** (also `Cmd+Z` or `Ctrl+Z`) reverses an **Archive**: the messages move back to where they were and the item comes back. After **Done** or **Mark done**, **Snooze** or a sent reply, Undo only steps the queue back to that item. What those did stays done: the item stays cleared, the mail stays snoozed and the reply stays sent. When the last item clears, you get a quiet "All caught up" screen.

## Detection and AI

Detection is two-stage:

1. A **deterministic heuristic** flags candidate threads: the latest inbound message puts you in To (not just Cc), the sender isn't a no-reply/bulk source (List-Unsubscribe, `Precedence: bulk`, no-reply addresses), and you haven't sent a later message in the thread.
2. With the `ai` feature flag enabled, a **cheap-tier AI pass** refines each candidate: whether it truly needs a reply, its urgency (which drives the ranking), the one-line ask summary, and any stated deadline.

The AI layer is advisory and fails soft — if it's disabled or errors, the deterministic queue still works with subjects as headlines and normal urgency. Message content is treated as untrusted data in the AI prompts, and the AI never sends or modifies mail on its own.

## Questions before the draft

Sometimes a good reply is blocked on **one fact only you can supply**: a yes/no decision, which date works, a price. Rather than draft a confident guess, Postbox can ask you first. When the AI pass decides a candidate reply hinges on such a fact, and a second, higher-quality confirmation agrees the answer is genuinely open (independently drafted replies disagree on it), the thread gets questions for you.

When that item opens in Answer mode, the questions come first, in a card above the editor ("One thing before I write this"). Each question offers:

- **Suggested answers** you can pick in one tap (e.g. *Yes* / *No*), plus
- **A free-text box** for your own answer. A question that asks for a file takes an upload or a file from Files instead.

The card says once, in your language, which email the questions came from ("Based on an email from `sender-domain`. Owlat never asks for your password."), and Owlat **deterministically drops** any question that fishes for a credential or one-time code. It will never relay a phishing prompt from inbound mail.

**Answer and draft** sends your answers. Owlat writes a starter reply that folds them in (and your learned writing voice, if enabled) and puts it in the editor. If you've already started writing, your text stays and the card offers **Use the draft** instead. **I'll answer later** puts the card away so you can just write. You always review and send yourself.

This whole loop is advisory and fails soft: with AI off, or if any step errors, there are simply no questions and the item opens like any other. Nothing here ever sends mail on its own.

## Draft-on-arrival — review & send

With the `postbox.aiDraft` flag on, Postbox writes the reply before you ask for one. The moment a message that needs a reply lands, or the moment you answer its questions, it **pre-generates a reply** for the thread, so you're never staring at a blank composer.

This runs the **same shared draft service** the AI agent uses on the B2B shared inbox: it drafts a grounded reply, then a second **quality self-check** scores the draft (completeness, grounding, tone-fit).

When the item opens in Answer mode, that reply is already in the editor, with a quiet **AI draft** tag and a **Discard** button next to it. If the draft left a detail open, the question card above it says so ("The draft needs one more detail"), and what you answer there goes into the draft.

**Human review only.** A prepared draft never sends on its own. You read, edit and send it in Answer mode. The whole thing fails soft: with AI off, no provider configured, a prompt-injection re-scan trip, or any generation error, no draft is written and the item opens with an empty reply. A newer message in the thread supersedes a stale draft rather than showing it.


## Reply reminders ("remind me if no reply")

The queue is about mail *you* owe a reply to. The mirror image — mail you're **waiting on a reply for** — is handled by reply reminders. Set one on a message you've sent and Owlat quietly watches the thread; if nobody replies by your deadline, it pulls the thread back to the top of your inbox so it doesn't slip.

This is deterministic — no AI involved.

You can arm a reminder two ways:

- **On send** — flip **Remind me if no reply** in the composer footer and pick a deadline. The reminder is armed on the message the moment it's sent.
- **After the fact** — open a sent message (or the thread it's in) and click the quiet **Remind me if no reply** button on the reader. It's only offered when the newest message in the thread is one you sent.

While a reminder is armed, the thread shows a **Reply reminder · &lt;date&gt;** chip; click its ✕ to cancel.

What happens at the deadline:

- **A reply arrived first** — any inbound message into the thread (except spam/trash-routed mail) clears the reminder silently. The reply you wanted came, so there's nothing to nag about.
- **No reply by the deadline** — the sweep resurfaces the thread **exactly once**: the watched message moves back into your **Inbox**, is marked **unread and flagged**, and the thread floats to the top. The reminder chip turns into an amber **No reply yet** state, and the thread is also added to the Answer queue as a **"You're waiting on &lt;name&gt;"** card. Dismiss it with **Done** on that card or the chip's ✕.

::callout{type="info" title="Feature flags"}
The Reply Queue ships with Postbox (`postbox` or `mail.external`). The ask summaries, urgency ranking, due hints, "Draft with AI", and the clarification questions require the `ai` feature flag; without it the queue is purely deterministic. **Draft-on-arrival** (the reply written before you open the item) additionally requires `postbox.aiDraft` — off by default, and forced off whenever `ai` is off or neither mailbox source (`postbox` or `mail.external`) is on. It works for connected external mailboxes the same as for hosted ones — but only for mail that arrives *after* the mailbox is connected. A **full-history import** is deliberately left out of the queue, so importing years of old mail never buries you in threads you answered long ago.
::
