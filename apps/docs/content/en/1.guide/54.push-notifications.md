Owlat can notify you in the browser, and in the installed web app on a phone or
desktop, even when no Owlat tab is open. It uses standard Web Push, the same
mechanism the browser uses for any site that sends notifications. The
[desktop app](/guide/desktop-app) has its own native notifications and does not
need this.

## Turning it on

Open **Preferences → This device → Push notifications** and click **Turn on**.
The browser asks whether Owlat may show notifications; Owlat never asks on its
own, only when you click. Each browser and each installed app is a separate
device, so turn it on wherever you want to hear from Owlat.

**Send test** next to a device sends a notification to that device right away,
so you can check that it arrives.

The card is missing when your server has no push keys configured. Ask whoever
runs your Owlat instance (see [Environment variables](/developer/environment-variables#optional-web-push-notifications)).

### iPhone and iPad

Apple only delivers Web Push to sites added to the Home Screen. In Safari, tap
**Share**, choose **Add to Home Screen**, open Owlat from the new icon, and turn
notifications on there. In a normal Safari tab the card shows this hint instead
of the **Turn on** button.

### Blocked notifications

If you said no to the browser's question, the browser remembers it and Owlat
cannot ask again. Allow notifications for your Owlat address in the browser's
site settings, then click **Turn on**.

## What notifies you

Push follows the same rules as the desktop app, so both always agree:

| Event                                                                           | When you are notified                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| New mail in your personal inbox                                                 | Follows **Notify me about** (**Everything**, **People & important only**, or **Nothing**). A muted conversation never notifies; a conversation set to **Alert on reply** always does. |
| A shared-inbox conversation assigned to you, or the agent asking you a question | Always, unless **Notify me about** is **Nothing**.                                                                                                                                    |
| A chat message that @-mentions you, or a direct message                         | Counts as mail from a person: notifies unless the room is muted or **Notify me about** is **Nothing**.                                                                                |

Mail you read in Owlat before the notification goes out, mail filed away by a
filter, and mail you sent yourself do not notify. Several messages in one
conversation replace each other, so a busy thread shows one notification
instead of a stack.

When an Owlat window is open and in front of you, no system notification is
shown: the app already shows the new mail, the assignment and the chat message
live. Clicking a notification opens the conversation, in the Owlat window you
already have open if there is one.

## Quiet hours

Quiet hours from **What notifies you** hold mail and chat notifications back
during the window you set. Each device uses its own time zone, so a laptop at
home and a phone abroad each go quiet at night where they are. When the window
ends, each device gets one summary ("3 notifications arrived during quiet
hours") instead of everything at once. As on the desktop, conversations set to
**Alert on reply** and conversations assigned to you still come through.

## Private notifications

Turn on **Private notifications** to keep mail and chat text off your lock
screen. Notifications then say only "New mail" or "New chat message", with no
sender, subject or text. The setting is shared with **Hide message previews** in
the desktop app, so it applies to every device.

Mail that arrived encrypted (see [Sealed Mail](/guide/sealed-mail)) never shows
its sender or subject in a notification, whatever this setting says.

## Your devices

**Devices that notify you** lists every browser and installed app with push
turned on, labelled like "Chrome on macOS", with when it was added and when it
last received a notification. **Remove** stops notifications to that device.
Owlat removes a device on its own when the browser reports the subscription is
gone, for example after you clear the site's data. Signing out turns
notifications off on that device, so the next person to use the browser does
not see yours.

## Privacy

A notification carries only what it displays: a sender name and a subject, a
chat author and the start of the message, or the generic line when private
notifications are on. It is encrypted end to end between your Owlat server and
the browser, so the push service that relays it (Google, Mozilla, Apple or
Microsoft, depending on the browser) cannot read it. Your account export lists
your notification devices by label and date; the subscription keys are not
exported.
