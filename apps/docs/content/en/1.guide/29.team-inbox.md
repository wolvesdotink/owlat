The Team Inbox is the shared mailbox where inbound email lands. Every reply, support request, or sales enquiry sent to one of your receiving addresses is stored, threaded by conversation, classified by the AI agent, and — depending on your [AI Agent](/guide/ai-agent) settings — given a suggested draft reply. Owners and admins triage from a single dashboard: read the thread, send or rewrite the agent's draft, work the Answer queue, and quarantine anything the security filter flags.

## What the Team Inbox is

The inbox is a _shared_ surface, not a per-user mailbox. There is one inbox for the workspace, and threads stay visible to every owner and admin so anyone on the team can pick up a conversation.

The inbox is gated behind the **Email inbox** feature flag (`inbox`), which is **off by default**. Until an operator enables it, the inbox routes are hidden and the backend queries refuse to return data. See [Feature flags](/guide/feature-flags) for how to turn it on, and note that receiving mail also requires the inbound MTA to be configured.

::callout{type="info" title="Where mail comes from"}
Inbound email reaches the inbox through your configured receiving channel — typically the bundled MTA forwarding to Owlat. SMS and WhatsApp threads can also surface here, and those channels are bidirectional — replies (both agent completions and manual admin sends) go back out the same channel; see [Communication Channels](/guide/channels).
::

## The inbox dashboard

The inbox landing page (**Inbox** in the dashboard) gives you a stats overview, status filters, and the thread list.

### Stats

A row of counters summarises the current state of the pipeline:

| Counter          | Meaning                                                                      |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Total**        | All inbound messages ever received.                                          |
| **Open Threads** | Conversations currently in the `open` status — your live backlog.            |
| **Drafts Ready** | Messages with an agent draft waiting for human review.                       |
| **Processing**   | Messages the agent is still working through.                                 |
| **Approved**     | Drafts approved and queued for sending.                                      |
| **Sent**         | Replies that have gone out.                                                  |
| **Quarantined**  | Messages held by the security filter.                                        |
| **Failed**       | Messages whose processing failed after the automatic retries were exhausted. |

The **Quarantined** and **Failed** counters are clickable, linking to the [Quarantine](#quarantine) and [Failed messages](#failed-messages) pages respectively.

The **Drafts Ready** count also appears as a badge on the **Review drafts** button so you can see your review backlog at a glance.

### Filtering the thread list

The thread list shows one row per conversation, newest activity first, with the sender, subject, message count, status badge, and time of the last message. Two filters narrow it down:

- **Status** — All Statuses, Open, Waiting, Resolved, or Closed.
- **Assigned to me** — only threads assigned to you.

Beside them, **Mentions** lists the threads where a teammate @-mentioned you in an [internal note](#internal-notes), newest mention first, with a badge for the ones you have not opened since. Use **Clear filters** to reset all of them. The list pages in batches; click **Load More** to fetch older threads.

## Reading a thread

Click any thread to open it. The thread detail view has a message timeline on the left and a sidebar on the right.

The **timeline** shows each inbound message oldest-first, with the sender, timestamp, subject, and full text body. Each message also carries a **processing status** badge — Received, Processing, Classified, Draft Ready, Approved, Sent, Quarantined, or Failed — so you can see where the agent got to.

### AI classification

When the agent has classified a message, a panel shows what it inferred:

| Field          | What it tells you                                                                                      |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Category**   | The kind of message (e.g. support, sales, billing, feature request, complaint, spam, internal, other). |
| **Priority**   | How urgent the agent judged it.                                                                        |
| **Sentiment**  | The tone it detected.                                                                                  |
| **Confidence** | How sure the agent is, as a percentage.                                                                |

The displayed classification panel doesn't auto-send a reply, but the category does drive pipeline routing: messages classified as spam are archived, and complaints or urgent messages are escalated straight to human review (skipping the auto-drafter). Auto-reply decisions are governed separately by the agent's confidence threshold and auto-reply settings, covered in [AI Agent & Autonomy](/guide/ai-agent).

### The sidebar

The right-hand sidebar shows the linked **Contact** (with a link to their full [contact profile](/guide/contacts)) when Owlat could match the sender, plus thread **Details**: status, message count, assignee, and the time of the last message.

## Replying

Every thread ends with a reply box. When the agent has drafted a reply (the message is in **Draft Ready**), the box opens pre-filled with the draft and its subject:

- **Review & send** — send the draft as it is. The message moves to **Approved**, and a short **undo window** (15 seconds by default — see [Undo after approving](#undo-after-approving)) opens before the reply actually leaves.
- Edit the text or the subject, and the box shows what changed against the agent's original. **Save draft** keeps your edit without sending; **Undo my changes** goes back to the agent's text.
- **Write my own** clears the box so you can start over.
- **Reject draft** discards it. A dialog lets you add an optional reason, which is recorded in the [audit log](/guide/audit-logs).

When there is no draft, the box is a single **Reply to …** line (press **r**) that opens an empty reply. A person can always write the reply: a message the agent failed on, one it never picked up (automated mail, or mail over the agent's rate limit), a draft someone rejected, or a message that was archived can all be answered from here. While the agent is still reading or drafting a message, or once a reply has gone out, the box says so instead.

If an earlier message in the thread also has a draft waiting, it is marked in the thread with **Answer this message** and **Reject draft**, so no draft is left behind or answered out of order.

::callout{type="tip" title="Save now, send later"}
**Save draft** keeps your revision without sending anything — the message stays in **Draft Ready** and shows a **Saved · edited by you** chip in the Answer queue. Edits are stored as **revisions**: the agent's original is always preserved, and the diff compares your current text against it, no matter how many times you save. Only when the reply actually sends (a send undone inside its window trains nothing), and only if the sent text differs from the agent's original, is one "edited" signal recorded for the agent's learning loop. Saving alone never trains the model.
::

## Answer queue

Drafts waiting for a person are reviewed in the **Answer queue** (`/dashboard/answer`). The Team inbox's **Review drafts** button opens it filtered to the team inbox (`?in=team`). It shows one message at a time — the sender, the original message, the classification and the agent's draft — with **Review & send**, **Reject**, **Edit in thread** and **Skip**. The old **Team drafts to review** page (`/dashboard/inbox/review`) redirects here; drafts are no longer approved or rejected in bulk.

### Undo after approving

A reply a person sends doesn't go out instantly: it is held for a short window (default **15 seconds**) with a countdown toast and an **Undo** button. Undo returns the message to **Draft Ready** with the draft intact. In the Answer queue, **Cmd+Z** does the same while the window is open. After the window closes the reply is on its way and cannot be recalled.

The window length is a per-instance agent setting (`humanApproveUndoDelayMs`, clamped to 0–120 seconds; 0 restores immediate sending — see [AI Agent & Autonomy](/guide/ai-agent#undo-window-for-human-approvals)). Autonomous auto-replies keep their own separate delay.

## Quarantine

Inbound mail passes through a security filter before the agent ever drafts a reply. Anything that trips the filter — prompt-injection attempts, suspected phishing, or other abuse — is held in **Quarantine** instead of being processed, so a poisoned message can never drive the agent.

Each quarantined message shows a **Security Alert** panel with:

- **Type** — what the scanner matched. The security scanner combines pattern matching with an LLM guard and emits three type values: `prompt_injection` (deterministic instruction-override patterns — a direct instruction-override attempt, a delimiter attack, or role impersonation in the message text), `llm_prompt_injection` (the guard-tier LLM classifier flagged it), and `smuggling:…` (hidden instructions inside the HTML body — e.g. `smuggling:html_comment`, `smuggling:invisible_text`, or `smuggling:zero_width_chars`).
- **Confidence** — how sure the scanner is, as a percentage. Messages are only quarantined above a high-confidence threshold.
- **Flagged content** — the specific snippet that tripped the filter.

You have two actions:

- **Release (False Positive)** — return the message to the pipeline. It re-enters at the start and is re-scanned and processed normally.
- **Block Sender** — add the sender's address to the suppression/blocklist and archive the message. Future inbound mail from that address is auto-archived on receipt and skips AI processing: it is still stored (never dropped — there is no SMTP-level rejection), but it lands directly in the archive instead of the classify/draft pipeline. The blocklist also suppresses future _outbound_ mail to that address.

::callout{type="warning" title="Release with care"}
Releasing a quarantined message hands its content to the agent. Only release messages you've confirmed are genuine — the quarantine exists precisely to keep untrusted content away from the model. For background on the scanning engine, see [Email Security](/developer/email-security).
::

## Failed messages

Processing can fail — a transient model error, a downstream timeout, or a bad payload. The pipeline retries automatically (up to three times), but a message that still can't make progress lands in a terminal **Failed** state and drops out of the workflow. The **Failed messages** page (reachable from the **Failed** stat card) surfaces every such message with its **failure reason** so an operator can read what went wrong.

Once the cause is resolved, **Retry processing** re-enqueues the message: it routes back to **Received** and the pipeline re-runs from the security scan. Retries are recorded in the [audit log](/guide/audit-logs).

## Assigning and resolving threads

Each thread carries a **status** that you change from the status dropdown in the thread header:

| Status       | Use it for                                      |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Open**     | An active conversation that needs attention.    |
| **Waiting**  | You've replied and are waiting on the customer. |
| **Resolved** | The issue is handled.                           |
| **Closed**   | Archived / done.                                |

Threads can also be **assigned** to a specific team member, which pairs with the **Assigned to me** filter on the inbox list so each person can see their own queue. Status changes and assignments are recorded in the [audit log](/guide/audit-logs).

## Discussing a thread in chat

If [Team Chat](/guide/chat) is enabled, you can pull a conversation into a channel for internal discussion without exposing it to the customer. From the thread header:

- If a channel already references this thread, it appears as a **#channel** link — click to jump straight there.
- Otherwise, **Discuss in channel** lets you spin up a new channel and link it to the thread.

A linked channel shows a pinned, read-only panel of the email thread to channel members who can read the Team Inbox, so they can talk it over internally; members without inbox access see the channel without the panel; customer-facing replies still go out through the inbox approval flow described above. The discuss controls only appear when the chat feature flag is on.

## Response targets

Response targets (an SLA) give every open conversation a reply deadline, so the team sees what is about to go unanswered. They are off until an owner or admin turns them on on the **Response targets** page (`/dashboard/inbox/response-targets`), which the inbox's **Analytics** page links to.

- **First response**: the target until the team has replied to the conversation once.
- **Next response**: the target after each new customer message once the team has replied.
- **What counts**: **Business hours** counts only the opening hours you set per weekday, in the time zone you choose, and skips the closed dates you list (holidays), so a target counts opening time: a 1-day target is 24 open hours, three 8-hour working days. A message that arrives at night is due counting from the next opening. **Every hour** counts around the clock.

The deadline is set when a customer message arrives and no reply is already owed. A second message from the customer does not move it: the oldest unanswered message sets the deadline. It ends when a reply goes out, whether you write it (by email, SMS, WhatsApp or chat), approve the agent's draft, the agent sends on its own, or you send a follow-up. Assigning, editing a draft or other internal steps do not count as a reply. Snoozing a conversation or setting it to **Waiting** pauses the deadline; it resumes with the time that was left. Resolving or closing a conversation ends the deadline, and so does the agent filing every new message away (informational mail, spam, quarantine). When you turn targets on, every open conversation whose customer is still waiting on an answer gets a deadline that starts at that moment (a conversation you already answered gets one with the customer's next message); changed targets apply to deadlines set from then on.

While targets are on, the inbox list shows the deadline on each row (**due in 2h**, **overdue 30m**) in place of the waiting time, turning amber within the last hour and red once passed. Two more filters, **Overdue** and **Due soon** (within the hour), sit beside the status tabs and work with the assignment filter, and the sort control offers **due first**. When a deadline passes unanswered, the assignee gets an in-app notice (and a desktop notification in the desktop app); an unassigned conversation notifies every owner and admin. Each deadline notifies once.

## Response analytics

**Analytics** on the inbox page opens how fast the team answers, for the conversations that started in a range you pick (the last 7, 30 or 90 days, or two dates up to a year apart):

| Number                | What it is                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Conversations**     | New conversations, also charted per day.                                                                                                         |
| **First response**    | Median time from the customer's first message to the first reply, with the time 90% of conversations were answered within. Also charted per day. |
| **Resolution**        | Median time from the first message to resolving or closing, for conversations resolved or closed now.                                            |
| **Replies on target** | Replies sent before their deadline, out of all replies measured against a target. A deadline that has already passed unanswered counts as late.  |

A table breaks the same numbers down per assignee (by the conversation's current assignee). Times are wall-clock time and days are UTC days. The first time someone opens the page, Owlat computes first-response and resolution times for older conversations in the background, so the page is not empty on day one; hit rates start when you turn targets on.

## Internal notes

A thread can carry **internal notes**: the team talking about the conversation inside it, where the customer never sees it. Notes appear in the timeline between the messages, in order of time, on a tinted card marked **Internal note** so nobody mistakes one for mail.

- **Add a note** from the end of the thread: **Add internal note** sits beside **Reply** (press **n**). In Answer mode, the **Internal note** tab beside **Reply** shows the thread's notes and a box to add one (also **n**); switching tabs keeps the reply you were typing.
- **Mention a teammate** with `@` followed by their handle (the part of their email address before the `@`). Typing `@` opens a picker that lists the people who can read the Team Inbox; nobody else can be mentioned. A mentioned teammate gets an in-app notice (and a desktop notification in the desktop app), and the thread shows up under their **Mentions** filter.
- **Edit or delete** your own notes from the note itself. An admin can delete anyone's note. A deleted note keeps its place in the timeline and reads **Note deleted**; its text is removed.
- The thread list shows how many notes a thread has, next to its status.

::callout{type="info" title="Notes never leave Owlat"}
Notes are kept apart from the mail. They are never sent: not in a reply, a quoted reply, a forward or a follow-up. They are not part of a contact's data export or any webhook payload, and the AI agent and the assistant never read them when drafting. They are included in the author's own [account export](/guide/account), and when a member's account is erased their notes stay on the thread without their name. When a contact is erased, the notes on that contact's threads are deleted with the threads.
::

Notes are plain text, up to 5,000 characters.

## Access control

The Team Inbox is restricted to **owners and admins**. The inbox queries use soft authentication: if you're signed in without one of those roles, the views load but stay empty rather than erroring. Members and other roles don't see inbox data at all. See [Team & Permissions](/guide/team-permissions) for the full role model.
